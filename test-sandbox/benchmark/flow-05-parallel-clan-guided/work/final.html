One CRM, twelve weeks, sixty-three thousand euro.

Lead-partner synthesis of three parallel specialist workstreams — market research, risk analysis and customer discovery — into a single costed recommendation and phased rollout for a 40-person Dublin advertising agency.

Executive summary

All three specialists independently recommended HubSpot — a rare unanimous verdict that makes the headline decision straightforward and shifts the lead-partner's job to costing it honestly and de-risking the rollout. HubSpot wins because it matches how Brightline actually works: native Meta and Google Ads sync with deal-level ROI attribution removes the duplicate-data-entry objection that would otherwise sink adoption among the eight media buyers; account managers reach working competence in one to two weeks on free HubSpot Academy material; creatives ride along on free seats; and no dedicated CRM admin is required — the single hardest constraint for a 40-person agency with no RevOps function.

Salesforce was rejected on cost and capacity grounds: roughly €79,000 a year in Enterprise licences before Marketing Cloud, premier support or the €20–50k implementation partner it effectively requires, plus the highest lock-in severity in the risk register and a 4–8 week learning curve the agency cannot absorb. Zoho was the genuine contender on price (~€19,200/yr) and the best raw EU residency story, but its headline saving is illusory for this buyer: near-mandatory premium support (20–25% of licence), Zapier/Make middleware to patch shallow ad-platform connectors, a thin Irish partner ecosystem, and an internal champion burning 2–3 days a month — all before counting the adoption-failure risk of dated UX in a culture where, as customer discovery put it, the tool either passes the pub-test in the first month or it dies.

Three-phase rollout — 12 weeks

PHASE 1

Foundation & Compliance

Weeks 1–4

Provision the portal in the EU (Frankfurt) data centre on day one — irreversible later, the risk-analyst's top HubSpot flag. Sign the DPA; configure consent and retention fields. Migrate only the last 12–18 months of client data. Onboard AMs and leadership, and move the weekly pipeline review onto the CRM dashboard immediately so the tool is the source of truth from week one.

PHASE 2

Ad-Ops Integration

Weeks 5–8

Switch on native Meta, Google Ads and LinkedIn connectors plus GA4. Onboard media buyers only after integrations demonstrably need zero duplicate typing — the decisive adoption lever for that role. Keep client campaign audiences in the ad platforms with non-marketing contact status by default, and contractually cap the marketing-contacts tier to kill the cost-creep risk both risk and discovery flagged.

PHASE 3

Full Adoption & Spreadsheet Retirement

Weeks 9–12

Creatives on free read-only seats from week 6 onward. Enforce the pre-announced day-60 kill-the-spreadsheet date. Track login rate, % of deals with next-step dates, and email-logging per AM weekly; coach rather than mandate for 30 days. Close with the half-day GDPR-handling refresher tied to CRM fields — compliance duty doubling as training reinforcement.

How the specialists were weighed

On the headline question there was no disagreement to break — market research, risk and discovery all landed on HubSpot at confidence 4/5. The real adjudication was in the tensions underneath. Zoho's price case (market-researcher: ~€19k vs ~€46k licences) and residency case (risk-analyst: best raw EU story, cheapest exit) had to be weighed against discovery's adoption evidence. I weighted adoption highest: the 500–800 staff-hour training delta between platforms is material for a firm that bills by the hour, and discovery's silent-non-use failure mode for Zoho would turn the ~€25k/yr licence saving into a written-off project. A CRM that is 60% cheaper and 50% used is the most expensive option on the table.

The one formally contested key after merge was assumptions: discovery's version carried team composition and stack detail; the risk-analyst's carried GDPR/PII scope and pricing caveats. They were complementary rather than contradictory, so I adjudicated by merging both into a superset rather than picking a winner — no specialist context was discarded. I also accepted both HubSpot caveats as binding conditions, not footnotes: Frankfurt provisioning is a Phase-1 gate, and the marketing-contacts cap goes in the contract. Standing trigger for revisiting the decision: if Brightline's marketing-contact count ever approaches ~50,000, the Zoho case re-opens, per the risk-analyst's explicit threshold.
