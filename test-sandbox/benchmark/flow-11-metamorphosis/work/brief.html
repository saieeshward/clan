Creative Brief · Confidential

Velvet Oat
The creamiest contradiction in Ireland.

Launch brief for Aurora Dairy's entry into the Irish oat-milk category

Client
Aurora Dairy
Market
Republic of Ireland
Budget
€120,000
Date
10 June 2026

01Background

Aurora Dairy is a third-generation Irish dairy co-operative headquartered in Tipperary, owned by roughly 1,400 farming families and best known for butter and fresh cream in every major Irish multiple. Liquid dairy volumes have been flat-to-declining for five years while plant-based alternatives grow double digits, led by oat.

Rather than watch imported brands own the category, the board has approved Velvet Oat: a premium, barista-grade oat milk made from Irish-grown oats — contracted growers within 80km of the Cahir plant — that wins blind taste tests on creaminess and foam stability. The risk is credibility: a dairy co-op selling oat milk invites accusations of bandwagon-jumping. The brand must feel like a confident act of craft, not a defensive line extension.

02Business Objective

Establish Velvet Oat as the #2 oat milk brand in the Irish off-trade within 12 months: 12% value share of the oat segment, €2.4m retail sales value, listings in all five major multiples plus 150 independent cafés pouring it as their house oat. Secondary: 65% prompted awareness in the core audience, with a measurable reputation halo back onto Aurora Dairy.

03Who We're Talking To

Irish adults 25–44, urban and commuter-belt, who already buy plant-based milk weekly but are not vegan — flexi-dairy shoppers with both oat and cow's milk in the fridge. Skews female, ABC1, coffee-literate. They buy on taste and texture first, ethics second, price third, and they warm strongly to genuine Irish provenance.

Primary Persona

Sorcha, 31 — UX designer, Dublin 8 (via Cork)

Spends €4 a day on flat whites; owns a €400 home espresso machine. She switched to oat for coffee three years ago because it tastes better in espresso, not because she gave up dairy — there is Aurora butter in her fridge right now. She buys Oatly out of habit but resents its shouty tone, and "oats from Sweden" on a carton in an Irish supermarket quietly annoys her.

Tension: she wants everyday choices to feel local and considered, but the plant-based aisle makes her choose between Irish identity and category credibility.

04Key Insight

Irish people didn't abandon dairy — they fell in love with coffee, and oat milk is simply what great coffee drinks now. The people who know milk best — Irish dairy farmers — are exactly the people you'd trust to make the creamiest oat milk. What looks like a contradiction is the strongest possible credential.

05Single-Minded Proposition

Velvet Oat: the creamiest oat milk in Ireland, made by the people who know creamy best.

06Mandatories

  • Velvet Oat identity and Aurora Dairy endorsement lock-up on all assets
  • "Irish-grown oats / grown within 80km" provenance claim in every execution
  • Barista performance (foam, stretch) demonstrated, not just claimed
  • No denigration of dairy — respect Aurora's core business and farmer-owners
  • Claims substantiated to ASAI standards; recyclable carton messaging where space allows
  • Irish-language tagline variant for TG4 and Gaeltacht OOH placements
