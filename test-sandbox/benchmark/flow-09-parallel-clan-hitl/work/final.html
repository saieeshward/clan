Brightline · CRM Platform Decision · Final · 10 June 2026

The verdict: HubSpot, re-scoped to fit a hard €40k ceiling

Three parallel workstreams — market research, risk analysis and customer discovery — evaluated Salesforce, HubSpot and Zoho CRM for a 40-person Dublin advertising agency. This document is the lead partner's final synthesis: one costed recommendation, a three-phase rollout, and the reasoning where the inputs pulled in different directions.

Executive summary

All three specialists independently recommended HubSpot — a convergence rare enough to be evidence in itself. The market researcher found it the best capability-to-adoption-to-cost balance, with native Meta and Google Ads sync and ROI attribution that map directly onto an ad agency's reporting workflow. The risk analyst found it the lowest aggregate residual risk: EU (Frankfurt) hosting selectable at portal creation, built-in GDPR tooling, moderate and manageable lock-in, transparent pricing. Customer discovery found it the lowest adoption risk by a wide margin: 6–8 hours to competence per head versus 20–30 for Salesforce, and free seats that defuse resentment among the 10 creatives.

Human-in-the-loop constraintA CEO directive was added by human edit during review: year-1 budget must not exceed €40,000. The market researcher's original model (25 Sales Pro seats + 15 lighter seats + Marketing Hub Pro) landed at €40–50k and would breach it. We treat the directive as binding and resolve it by re-scoping, not by switching vendor.

The costed recommendation

Brightline buys paid seats only where they earn their keep: 12 account managers and 3 leadership/finance staff on Sales Hub Professional. The 8 media buyers work through Marketing Hub's ads tooling and view access; the 10 creatives take free seats for brief and client visibility. Client campaign audiences stay in the ad platforms — never imported as marketing contacts — which both holds the Marketing Hub base tier and answers the risk analyst's top HubSpot concern (contact-tier price spikes, severity 3).

How the specialists were weighed where they disagreed

The contested key. The merge flagged one conflict: the assumptions field, where the customer-discovery and risk-analyst framings collided and last-write policy had picked customer discovery. Neither was wrong — they were complementary. I adjudicated by merging them: customer discovery's staffing and stack detail, the risk analyst's GDPR/client-PII scoping (it materially shaped the EU-hosting requirement), and the human-edited CEO budget cap, recorded as binding.

Budget vs unanimity. The real tension was the CEO cap against the market researcher's €40–50k HubSpot model. The cheap answer was Zoho at ~€19.2k in licences. I rejected it because the other two specialists' findings erode that saving: the risk analyst rates Zoho's standard support near-unusable for business-critical work (premium support adds 20–25%), flags connector gaps that force middleware spend, and notes thinner DPA maturity that complicates client-side GDPR audits — a real cost for an agency whose clients audit their processors. Customer discovery adds that Zoho adoption is champion-dependent, costing an internal champion 2–3 days a month indefinitely, and that media buyers would find its ad-platform integrations clunky — the exact group whose resistance sinks agency CRMs. A unanimous three-way recommendation should only be overturned by a constraint that cannot be engineered around; this one could be. Down-scoping to 15 paid seats keeps HubSpot at ~€35,800, inside the cap.

Salesforce. Eliminated without much contest: ~€79k/year in Enterprise licences alone before Marketing Cloud, implementation partners (€20–50k one-off) or the 0.5–1 FTE admin Brightline does not have. The risk analyst's two severity-4 entries (lock-in, implementation overrun) both attach to Salesforce, and customer discovery's training delta — 500–800 staff-hours in year one versus HubSpot — is material for a firm that bills by the hour.

Residual risks I am accepting. Marketing-contact tier creep (mitigated by the contractual cap and keeping audiences in ad platforms); the irreversibility of the EU data-residency choice (mitigated by making Frankfurt provisioning the literal first task of Phase 1); and per-seat cost growth if Brightline scales past ~50 staff or 50k marketing contacts, at which point the Zoho case reopens — both the risk analyst and I flag that explicitly as the review trigger.

Three-phase rollout — 12 weeks

1

Foundation

WEEKS 1–4

Provision the EU (Frankfurt) portal on day one and verify before any import. Sign the DPA, obtain the sub-processor list. Migrate only the last 12–18 months of client data. Onboard 12 AMs + 3 leadership via HubSpot Academy. Move the weekly pipeline review into HubSpot immediately; announce the 60-day kill-the-spreadsheet date.

2

Ad-ops integration

WEEKS 4–8

Switch on native Meta, Google Ads and LinkedIn integrations; stand up the DV360 cost connector. Onboard the 8 media buyers only once the integrations demonstrably need zero duplicate typing. Appoint two champions (one AM, one buyer) with 10% protected time. Audiences stay in ad platforms.

3

Full adoption

WEEKS 8–12

Creatives join on free seats. Spreadsheets retire at day 60. Half-day GDPR refresher tied to consent and retention fields. Weekly metrics: login rate, % deals with next-step dates, email-logging per AM — coaching, not mandates, for the first 30 days. Monthly check of marketing-contact count against the cap.
