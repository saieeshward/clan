Executive summary

Brightline should adopt HubSpot Sales Hub Professional on a 25-paid-seat basis, at an estimated all-in year-one cost of €45,500. The decisive factor is not features or price — it is that adoption risk is the dominant failure mode for agency CRM rollouts, and HubSpot wins that dimension outright: 1–2 weeks to per-user proficiency versus 4–8 for Salesforce, ~10–14 training hours per account manager versus 24–32, no dedicated admin required, and an EMEA HQ in Dublin that puts local support, user groups, and a hiring pool on Brightline's doorstep.

HubSpot is also the only one of the three with first-party Meta/Google ads integrations at mid-tier — the campaign-to-pipeline attribution loop an ad agency must demonstrate to its own clients. Salesforce (~€79k licences alone, plus €15–40k implementation and a 0.5-FTE admin Brightline does not have) concentrates cost, lock-in, and shelfware risk a 40-person agency cannot absorb; the risk register's only severity-5 entry sits against it. Zoho (~€19k) is the legitimate value pick, but its ~€26k saving is repaid in configuration labour, weaker ads tooling, slower support, and moderate adoption resistance — a poor trade against the cost of a stalled rollout.

Two conditions are non-negotiable: the EU (Frankfurt) data region must be selected at account creation (it cannot be retrofitted — this is a go/no-go gate before any data import), and a monthly contact-tier hygiene policy must run from day one to neutralise HubSpot's contact-count pricing escalation. All figures are knowledge-only estimates (model cutoff early 2026, no web verification, ±20%); written quotes are required before signature.

Three-phase rollout

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–6

Foundation & pilot

Sign a 1-year term (no auto-uplift). Gate: provision the account in the EU (Frankfurt) region and verify it in settings before any import — this cannot be retrofitted. Map consent/lawful-basis fields, run a DPIA-lite review, dedupe the spreadsheets, then wave-1 import (contacts & companies only). Pilot with one full account pod — 1 AM lead, 2 AMs, 1 media buyer — for four weeks. Switch on Gmail/calendar auto-capture so the CRM fills itself, and enforce a max-7-required-fields policy on every record.

Phase 2 · Weeks 7–14

Core rollout

All 12 account managers go live; wave-2 import brings deals and pipeline. The exec sponsor (MD or Head of Client Services) runs the Monday pipeline meeting exclusively from the CRM — the single strongest adoption lever identified. HubSpot Academy certifications happen in paid time (~10–14h per AM). Connect the native Meta and Google Ads integrations, and appoint one champion per discipline with two protected hours per week.

Phase 3 · Weeks 15–26

Attribution & scale

Media buyers onboard to read/report views only (~4h each); creatives never enter the CRM — briefs flow to the existing PM tool. Stand up programmatic (DV360/Trade Desk) reporting via middleware. Take the gated Marketing Hub decision. Run the monthly contact-tier hygiene audit, schedule the annual export drill, and hold the day-90 kill-or-commit review: ≥90% of live deals in the CRM, activity logged within 24h, forecasts built only from CRM data.

Where the specialists disagreed — and how it was resolved

Tension 1 · Licence basis

Market research costed 25–30 paid seats (~€43.2k); customer discovery argued creatives should hold zero seats and buyers read-only views. Resolution: 25 paid seats. The role-level adoption analysis is more granular than the headline pricing assumption, so it governs seat design — cutting the licence line to €27k and roughly a third of apparent training burden — while the researcher's per-seat price is retained.

Tension 2 · GDPR residency

The risk analyst rated Zoho's default EU residency (its own Dublin/Amsterdam data centres) strongest, while HubSpot's Frankfurt region must be chosen at signup and cannot be switched later. Resolution: HubSpot, with residency as a hard Phase-1 gate. The residual risk is Brightline-side configuration, not vendor posture; a defaults advantage does not outweigh a decisive adoption and ad-ops lead when the mitigation is a one-time verified checkbox.

Tension 3 · The Zoho budget case

Zoho saves ~€26k in year one. Resolution: rejected. Two of three specialists independently identified adoption as the binding constraint; a stalled CRM costs far more than the saving, and Zoho's thinner ads integration undercuts the core use case — campaign-linked revenue reporting. Cost was weighted second to adoption throughout.

Tension 4 · Marketing Hub vs contact-tier costs

Market research treated Marketing Hub Pro as a natural add-on; the risk analyst flagged contact-count pricing escalation as a severity-3 trap for an agency holding large audience lists. Resolution: both accepted — defer and gate. Marketing Hub is a Phase-3 decision budgeted at six months, paired with monthly contact hygiene (mark non-marketing contacts, archive stale lists quarterly) from day one.
