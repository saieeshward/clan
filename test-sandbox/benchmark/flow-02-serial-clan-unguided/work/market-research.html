Salesforce vs HubSpot vs Zoho CRM for a 40-seat Dublin agency

Comparative capabilities, EUR pricing, and ad-ops integration fit. Prepared by the market-researcher agent for handoff to risk analysis (GDPR, lock-in, adoption risk).

Assumptions & caveats. Produced without web access from model knowledge, cutoff January 2026. Figures are vendor list prices in EUR, annual billing, ex-VAT, and must be re-verified before contracting. HubSpot's 2024 shift to seat-based pricing and Salesforce's 2023/2025 list-price rises (~9% each) make quotes especially perishable. We model 40 full seats; real deployments often mix full and lighter licenses, cutting cost 20–35%.

Capabilities & feature strengths

Salesforce is the depth leader: custom objects, Flow automation, enterprise reporting, and the AppExchange ecosystem are unmatched. For an agency, that depth buys multi-entity pipeline modelling (brands, campaigns, retainers) — but it demands a dedicated admin or a partner retainer, and most of its ad-ops muscle sits in paid add-ons rather than Sales Cloud itself.

HubSpot is the usability and marketing-native leader. Its built-in Ads tool syncs Meta, Google, and LinkedIn campaigns directly into the CRM, supports CRM-list audience targeting, and attributes ad spend to closed revenue out of the box — uniquely relevant for an ad agency that wants to dogfood client reporting. Custom objects and advanced permissioning are gated to Enterprise, so complex data models get expensive.

Zoho CRM covers ~80% of HubSpot's functionality at ~45% of the price: solid workflow automation, decent customisation, native Google Ads integration, and EU data centres. The trade-offs are a clunkier UI, a weaker Meta/programmatic story, and a thinner Irish partner and hiring market.

Preliminary read (pre-risk-analysis)

HubSpot is the provisional front-runner: the only platform with native Meta/Google ad-spend attribution, roughly half Salesforce's year-1 cost, and the lowest adoption friction for a creative-heavy team. Zoho is the disciplined value alternative if budget dominates and Meta integration can run through middleware. Salesforce is justified only if Brightline anticipates enterprise pipeline complexity or buys Data Cloud for programmatic activation. The risk-analyst should now weigh GDPR posture, contact-tier cost creep (HubSpot), vendor lock-in and price-rise exposure (Salesforce), and ecosystem/talent thinness (Zoho).
