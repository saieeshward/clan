Stage 2 · Risk Analysis

Risk Analysis — GDPR, Lock-in, Migration, Hidden Costs, Support

Knowledge-only assessment (cutoff Jan 2026). DPF status, sub-processor lists and contract terms must be re-verified against vendor DPAs and trust pages before signature.

GDPR posture & EU data residency

Salesforce LOW–MODEU residency via Hyperforce; EU Operating Zone (extra cost) adds EU-located support. US parent keeps FISA 702 / CLOUD Act reachability regardless of residency. DPF-certified, SCC fallback, mature DPA and audit stack (ISO 27001/27701, SOC 2). Large but well-documented sub-processor list.
HubSpot LOW–MODFrankfurt (AWS eu-central-1) hosting must be chosen at portal creation — later relocation is effectively a re-implementation. US parent, DPF-certified, SCCs in DPA; some support/telemetry access can originate outside the EU. Good sub-processor transparency.
Zoho MODERATEZoho-owned Amsterdam + Dublin data centres (no hyperscaler dependency — a plus). Indian parent has no EU adequacy decision: India-based support access rests on SCCs, and sub-processor/audit documentation is thinner. Request EU-only support handling and run a TIA.

Shared assumption: the EU–US Data Privacy Framework remains valid as of Jan 2026 but is litigation-exposed (Schrems-pattern). With EU residency plus SCC fallback, all three are viable for an Irish controller holding B2B contact data (no special-category data).

Vendor lock-in & exit cost

Salesforce is the deepest trap: data egress is easy (APIs, weekly export) but process egress — Apex/Flow automations and metadata — is a EUR 15–40k rebuild, on top of multi-year non-cancellable orders. HubSpot exports CRM objects cleanly, but marketing workflows, email assets and attribution history do not port, and annual contracts carry auto-renewal uplift clauses. Zoho has the lightest contractual lock-in, but the Zoho One bundle is an operational lock-in trap: once email, finance and projects ride on it, exit cost multiplies across the business — keep scope CRM-only for the first 12 months.

Migration risk

Inbound migration (assumed: spreadsheets / lightweight legacy CRM) is technically low-complexity everywhere; HubSpot has the best guided tooling, Salesforce realistically requires a partner (cost already modelled), Zoho is fine until something stalls — and the thin Irish partner pool slows the rescue. The dominant migration risk is human, not technical: if 40 creatives abandon the tool, the CRM is stale within a quarter. Adoption risk ranks Salesforce worst, HubSpot best.

Hidden-cost risk

Salesforce: ~9% list-rise cadence (2023, 2025 — assume recurrence), paid ad-ops add-ons, Premier Support (~30% uplift) and an admin retainer push realistic 3-year TCO to 1.5–2× the EUR 79.2k/yr headline. HubSpot: the marketing-contact tier is the trap — cost scales with database size, not seats; an agency importing prospect lists can double the EUR 9.5–19k/yr add-on, and custom objects sit behind an Enterprise upgrade (~EUR 150/seat). Zoho: the headline price is honest, but budget EUR 5–10k/yr in middleware and developer glue to fill the Meta/programmatic gaps.

Support risk

HubSpot is the standout for Brightline: EMEA HQ in Dublin, phone/chat support included at Pro tier, deepest local agency-partner bench — LOW. Salesforce standard support is slow; meaningful SLAs cost extra, but the Irish partner ecosystem is excellent — LOW with a retainer, MODERATE without. Zoho routes support through an EU/India mix, premium support adds ~20–25%, and local partners are scarce — MODERATE–HIGH; Brightline would be largely self-reliant.

Risk verdict: the preliminary ranking HubSpot > Zoho > Salesforce survives the stress-test. HubSpot's dominant risks (contact-tier creep, renewal uplift) are contract-mitigable and its GDPR + Dublin support posture fit best. Zoho's savings carry recurring taxes: middleware glue, support self-reliance, heavier GDPR diligence. Salesforce carries the highest composite risk for capability Brightline does not yet need.
