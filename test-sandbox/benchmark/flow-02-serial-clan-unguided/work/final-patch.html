Stage 3 · Final Recommendation — Lead Partner

Decision: HubSpot, EU-hosted, €70k year one

Executive summary

Adopt HubSpot — Sales Hub Professional + Marketing Hub Professional, 40 seats, Frankfurt residency.

Brightline should sign HubSpot at an estimated year-1 total of ~€70,000 (range €62–78k, ex-VAT). It is the only platform of the three with native Meta/Google/LinkedIn ad sync, CRM-audience targeting and ad-spend-to-revenue attribution out of the box — the exact capability an ad agency sells — at roughly two-thirds of Salesforce’s realistic year-1 cost and with the lowest adoption risk for a 40-person creative team. Its two material risks (marketing-contact tier creep, renewal auto-uplift) are both contract-mitigable, and its GDPR posture (Frankfurt hosting, DPF + SCCs) plus Dublin-based EMEA support are the best fit of the three for an Irish controller. Salesforce is rejected: highest composite risk (adoption 4, cost-creep 4, lock-in 3) buying depth Brightline does not yet need. Zoho is rejected as primary but retained as the documented budget fallback: its €26k headline hides €5–10k/yr of middleware glue on the agency’s core Meta workflow, moderate GDPR diligence overhead, and support self-reliance. This recommendation rests on Jan-2026 knowledge-cutoff list pricing; a live-quote verification gate is mandatory before signature (Phase 1).

Non-negotiable contract terms (from the risk register)

  • EU residency at portal creation. Frankfurt hosting specified in the order form and the portal region verified in writing before any data import — it cannot be relocated later (sev-3).
  • Written contact-tier pricing schedule appended to the order form, neutralising the sev-4 tier-creep risk.
  • Renewal uplift cap (CPI or fixed %) with the renewal notice window calendared (sev-2).
  • Live-quote price verification of all Jan-2026 list figures before signature (sev-3, all vendors).

How the specialists were weighed

Both specialists converged on HubSpot > Zoho > Salesforce, so the ranking itself was not contested — but three tensions between stage 1 and stage 2 required adjudication. First, Zoho’s value case. The researcher presented Zoho as a disciplined value play at less than half HubSpot’s cost. The risk analyst’s findings outweigh this: the €5–10k/yr middleware tax lands on Meta sync — the centre of an ad agency’s workflow — and is rated sev-4 with explicit fragility. Combined with moderate-high support risk (thin Irish partner pool) and SCC-only India-parent transfers requiring a TIA, roughly half the saving evaporates and what remains buys operational fragility. I weighted the risk view heavier because recurring operational taxes compound while a purchase-price gap is paid once a year and shrinks under seat-mix optimisation.

Second, Salesforce’s capability depth. Stage 1 correctly noted Salesforce is the only platform with best-in-class programmatic activation — if Data Cloud is purchased. Stage 2 rated its composite risk highest of the three. The risk view prevails on a simple test: nothing in Brightline’s brief evidences enterprise pipeline complexity today, and paying ~€104k+ year 1 plus a retainer for optionality, against a sev-4 adoption-collapse risk in a creative team, is a poor trade. The reverse-ETL contingency in Phase 3 buys 80% of the programmatic outcome for ~5% of the cost. Third, the cost number itself. Stage 1’s chart showed HubSpot at ~€66k year 1 using a mid-tier contact estimate; stage 2 flagged contact-tier creep as sev-4 for agencies that import prospect lists. I credited the risk finding and moved the marketing line to the upper-mid tier, revising the headline to ~€70k with the €62–78k band — a deliberately conservative figure so the contract-term mitigations, not optimism, carry the downside.

Standing assumptions inherited and accepted: Jan-2026 knowledge-cutoff pricing (hence the Phase 1 gate); DPF valid but litigation-exposed with SCC fallback contracted in every DPA; B2B contact data only, no special-category data; 40 paid seats as modelled.
