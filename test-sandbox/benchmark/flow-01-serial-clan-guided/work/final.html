Brightline · CRM Platform Selection · Final Decision Memo

HubSpot, governed. Salesforce, eliminated. Zoho, on the bench.

Final recommendation synthesising market research (hop 1) and risk analysis (hop 2) for a 40-person Dublin advertising agency. One costed pick, a 14-week rollout, and the reasoning behind the call.

01Executive summary

The decision. Brightline should standardise on HubSpot Sales Hub Professional. At a realistic mixed-seat configuration (18 paid seats for account and new-business roles; everyone else on free view-only seats) the licence cost is €19,440/year, and the fully loaded year-1 cost — including HubSpot's onboarding fee, data migration and dedupe, training, a Supermetrics-class ETL layer, and a 15% price contingency — is €33,576. That is roughly a third of Salesforce's licence-only list price and buys the lowest adoption risk of the three platforms, the best native Meta Ads / Google Ads / GA4 integration, Frankfurt EU hosting, and the practical advantage of HubSpot's EMEA HQ being in Dublin.

What was eliminated and why. Salesforce Enterprise is removed from consideration: €79,200 list becomes a realistic €95–110k year-2 TCO once an admin/partner retainer, ads connectors, and 7–10% renewal uplifts land — a severity-5 hidden-cost risk compounded by severity-4 adoption and lock-in risks, with no proportional capability gain at 40 seats. Zoho CRM Enterprise (€19,200) is retained as the named fallback: it is honest, cheap, and has the best pure data-residency story (Dublin DC), but its support thinness in Ireland forces a year-1 premium-support spend of 20–25% of licence, which erases most of its price advantage against HubSpot's mixed-seat cost.

Agency-wide Rollout

05How the specialists were weighed

Where they agreed, I simply ratified. Both hops independently converged on the same shortlist — HubSpot mixed-seat and Zoho Enterprise — and the same elimination of Salesforce. The market researcher made the economic case (3–4× cost without proportional value at 40 seats); the risk analyst made it terminal (severity-5 hidden-cost risk, €95–110k realistic TCO, €15–30k exit rework). Two independent lenses reaching the same kill decision is the strongest signal in this pipeline, and I treat it as settled.

Where they diverged, I sided with the risk analyst — with one numerical correction. The researcher left HubSpot vs Zoho as an open tie on the cost-capability frontier; the risk analyst broke it for HubSpot. I adopted that ordering because at near-identical licence prices (€19,440 vs €19,200) the tie-breakers are risk-shaped, not price-shaped: HubSpot's two material risks (seat creep, contact-tier creep) are governable by internal policy at zero cash cost, whereas Zoho's material risk (thin Irish support and partner bench) can only be mitigated by spending money — premium support at 20–25% of licence plus €2–6k of integration glue, lifting Zoho's true year-1 cost to roughly €30k and erasing its €240 headline advantage. A risk you can write a policy against beats a risk you must pay down annually.

I also overruled the cheapest option on the table. Zoho One (€17,760) is the lowest sticker price in the entire evaluation, and the researcher floated it as a realistic tier. I rejected it on the risk analyst's bundle-entanglement finding: adopting the suite widens exit cost from €5–10k to €15–25k and couples CRM replacement to Books/Desk/Campaigns. The cheapest licence that maximises lock-in is not the cheapest option.

Caveats carried forward intact. All figures are knowledge-cutoff (early 2026) list prices, ±10–15%, with no web verification performed at any hop — hence the 15% contingency line inside the costed total, not as a footnote. Validate against live vendor quotes before contracting. GDPR is a manageable obligation on all three vendors, not a differentiator: the residual obligations (RoPA, consent basis for audience syncing, joint-controller documentation) sit with Brightline regardless of platform and are scheduled into Phases 1 and 3.
