CRM Evaluation: Final Recommendation & Rollout Plan

Executive summary

Recommendation: adopt HubSpot (Sales Hub Professional + Marketing Hub Professional), hosted in the EU data centre (Frankfurt), at an estimated year-1 total cost of approximately €68,000. HubSpot offers the best capability-per-euro for an advertising agency: native Meta/Google Ads integration, the lowest adoption risk across a 40-person team where many staff are creatives, Dublin-based EMEA support, and residual risks that are all mitigable by contract and configuration rather than structural. We recommend a 12-week, three-phase rollout beginning with an EU-resident portal, a data-cleanse sprint and a 10-user sales pilot, expanding to ad-ops integrations after a documented consent review, and reaching agency-wide use by week 12.

The decision and how it was reached

Both specialist workstreams converged on the same direction, which makes the headline decision straightforward: the market analysis rated HubSpot the strongest capability-per-euro fit for agency workflow, and the risk analysis confirmed that HubSpot’s residual risks are mitigable while Salesforce’s dominant risks (cost blow-out at 1.5–2.0× licence in year 1, adoption failure among non-sales staff, configuration lock-in — all rated severity 4) are structural at 40 seats. Zoho remains a credible budget hedge at roughly a third of the licence cost, but only with premium support and middleware priced in, and it is weakest exactly where Brightline needs strength: ad-ops integration and EU partner depth.

Where the specialists were in tension, I weighed them as follows:

  • Ad-ops integration vs. GDPR exposure. The market researcher praised HubSpot’s native audience syncing; the risk analyst flagged the same feature as the single highest cross-vendor risk (joint-controller exposure, severity 4). I sided with the risk analyst on sequencing, not on substance: the integration is a core reason to choose HubSpot, but it is gated to Phase 2 behind a consent/legitimate-interest review and RoPA documentation. The risk attaches to the activity, not the vendor — it would exist on any platform.
  • Seat count. The market researcher costed all 40 staff but noted ~25 paid seats plus free/read-only seats is realistic. I costed conservatively on the full 40-seat blended figure (€53,000/year) so the budget holds even if every member of staff needs a paid seat; the ~25-seat model is an identified saving (€15–20k) to pursue at contract, not a number to bank in advance.
  • Sticker price vs. true cost. The risk analyst’s hidden-cost multipliers (HubSpot 1.2–1.5× licence) outweigh list-price comparisons. My €68,000 estimate sits at ~1.28× licence, inside that band, whereas Salesforce at the same multiplier logic lands at €95–150k+ in year 1 — disqualifying at this headcount.
  • Zoho’s price advantage. A ~€34k annual saving is real, but the risk register shows it eroded by premium support (~20–25% of licence), middleware to close ad-ops gaps, and a thin EU partner bench (severity 3) that could strand a stalled rollout. For an agency whose revenue depends on campaign operations, I judged integration fit and support proximity worth the premium.

Three-phase rollout plan (12 weeks)

Phase 1 — Foundation & pilot (weeks 1–4)

Create the portal with the EU data centre selected on day one (region is fixed at creation — risk #2); execute DPA with SCCs and a transfer impact assessment; validate the Professional-tier permission model during trial before signing (risk #10). Run the data dedup/cleanse sprint on existing spreadsheets, then migrate contacts and deals in waves with validation gates. Pilot with ~10 sales and account-management users; configure core pipeline, deal stages and reporting. Negotiate annual (not multi-year) terms and the seat/contact-tier structure.

Phase 2 — Sales rollout & ad-ops integration (weeks 5–8)

Extend to all client-facing staff. Complete the consent/legitimate-interest review and document audience syncing in the RoPA before enabling Meta/Google Ads integrations (risk #4 — severity 4). Connect lead-ad capture and ad-spend attribution; stand up middleware for GA4/UTM reporting and any programmatic audience flows, keeping that logic outside vendor-native automation to preserve exit options. Cap and archive cold contacts to control Marketing Hub tier creep.

Phase 3 — Agency-wide adoption & governance (weeks 9–12)

Roll out to remaining staff (creatives/ops) with lightweight or free-seat access where viable — this is where the ~€15–20k seat-optimisation saving is realised. Launch Marketing Hub campaigns, dashboards and management reporting; deliver role-based training; institute quarterly full data exports and a renewal-negotiation calendar entry for contact tiers. Hold a post-rollout governance review at week 12 against adoption and pipeline-hygiene KPIs.

Assumptions and caveats

All figures are knowledge-based estimates (January 2026 cutoff, no live vendor quotes), annual billing, ex-VAT, assuming rough EUR/USD parity. Residency options, certification claims and contract norms must be verified in the DPA and order form before signature. Brightline is assumed to be migrating from spreadsheets/legacy tooling and to act as data controller. Obtain a current HubSpot quote and a Salesforce Pro Suite counter-quote before contract to pressure-test pricing.
