Brightline CRM Evaluation: Final Recommendation

Prepared 10 June 2026 · 40-person advertising agency, Dublin, Ireland · Year-1 budget cap: EUR 40,000 (CEO directive) · Inputs: market-researcher, customer-discovery, risk-analyst specialist reports

Executive summary

Recommendation: adopt HubSpot (Sales Hub Professional for the 15 revenue-facing seats, Marketing Hub Professional, free core seats for the remaining 25 users), hosted in the EU (Frankfurt) data region selected at portal creation. Estimated year-1 total cost: EUR 34,300 (EUR 37,700 with 10% contingency) — inside the EUR 40,000 cap.

All three specialists independently recommended HubSpot, each at confidence 4/5, from three different lenses: it is the only platform with genuinely native Meta/Google/LinkedIn ad-account sync and ad-attributed revenue reporting (market research); it has the shallowest learning curve, free role-based training, and needs no admin headcount (adoption); and it carries the best overall risk balance — EU hosting, moderate lock-in, good included support (risk). Salesforce is over-scaled for 40 seats (~EUR 79k/yr licences alone, plus EUR 15–50k implementation — roughly double the cap before training) and carries the deepest adoption dip. Zoho is the documented budget fallback (~EUR 24k/yr) but adds integration assembly, the weakest support, and the most compliance-documentation work.

Where the specialists disagreed, and how I weighed them

The vendor choice was unanimous, so my reconciliation work was on three subtler tensions:

1. Cost vs. the budget cap. The market researcher's HubSpot estimate ranged EUR 30–48k/yr — the upper end breaches the CEO's EUR 40k directive. The breach scenario assumes broad paid seating or high marketing-contact tiers. I resolved this by fixing the configuration to the researcher's own mixed-seat model: 15 paid Sales Hub Pro seats (the ~12 AMs plus new-business/ops leads identified by customer discovery), Marketing Hub Pro at the ≤2k-contact entry tier, and the ~25 creatives/passive users on free core seats — exactly matching the adoption finding that creatives should be read-only and out of mandatory workflows. This converts an adoption recommendation into a cost control. The risk analyst's "contact-tier cost creep" risk (severity 3) is the main threat to this number; mitigation (non-marketing contact flagging, 3-year contact-growth modelling, annual tier negotiation) is built into Phase 1.

2. GDPR: Salesforce is formally stronger. The risk analyst rates Salesforce's compliance posture best (residual risk: low) versus HubSpot's low-medium (US parent, DPF reliance). I weighed this below cost and adoption because (a) the residual gap is closable with the analyst's own mitigations — EU Frankfurt residency at portal creation, a written transfer impact assessment, SCC fallback in the DPA — and (b) Salesforce's compliance edge is worthless if the licence cost alone is double the total budget and adoption stalls without an admin the agency cannot staff. A control that nobody uses protects nothing.

3. Zoho as the value play. At ~EUR 24k/yr Zoho beats HubSpot on price by ~EUR 10k. I rejected it as primary because three independent costs erode the saving: higher first-year training (EUR 5–12k vs EUR 2–5k), middleware assembly for Meta lead ads and GA4, and the weakest support tier with shadow-spreadsheet adoption risk. Net of those, the gap shrinks toward zero while accepting medium compliance residual risk. Zoho remains the formal fallback if budget pressure tightens.

Three-phase rollout plan

Phase 1 — Foundation & pipeline core Weeks 1–4

  • Create portal with EU (Frankfurt) data hosting; verify region in account settings before any data import (irreversible — risk analyst severity 4).
  • Sign DPA with SCC fallback; complete transfer impact assessment; flag non-marketing contacts from day one.
  • Appoint two champions (one senior AM, one media buyer) with config ownership and a weekly 30-minute office hour for 8 weeks.
  • Migrate live clients and last 12 months of history only; archive the rest. Onboard the 12 AMs; HubSpot Academy paths (2–6 hrs/person).
  • Set three non-negotiable behaviours: every client interaction logged, pipeline updated weekly, no side spreadsheets — modelled visibly by leadership.

Phase 2 — Media buyers & ad-ops integration Weeks 5–8

  • Connect Meta, Google, and LinkedIn ad accounts via the native Ads tool; enable lead-form capture and ad-attributed revenue reporting.
  • Add GA4 connector; assess any DSP middleware needs from the EUR 2,000 reserve.
  • Onboard the 8 media buyers; week-4 adoption checkpoint (weekly active users, % deals updated in last 7 days).

Phase 3 — Read-only rollout & adoption verification Weeks 9–12

  • Give creatives free-seat read-only access to briefs and client history; no mandatory workflows.
  • Week-8 gate: <70% AM weekly-active triggers intervention (re-training, workflow simplification) before Phase 3 completes.
  • Stand up the quarterly full-data export to Brightline-controlled storage (exit hedge); week-12 review of adoption metrics and contact-tier consumption against the 3-year cost model.

Decision record

Final recommendation: HubSpot, EUR 34,300 year-1 (EUR 37,700 with contingency), confidence 4/5 — capped by pricing uncertainty inherent to a knowledge-only pipeline, not by disagreement among inputs. Fallback: Zoho CRM Enterprise if the budget cap is reduced below ~EUR 30k. Salesforce eliminated: cost roughly 2× cap before training, requires admin capacity Brightline does not have.
